Scrubbing systems

How a wet scrubber cleans acid fumes

Inside a packed-bed scrubber: what happens to the fumes between the inlet and the clean air at the top.

Plasto-Fab team · 2 min read

Plants that handle acids, solvents and other aggressive chemicals give off fumes that have to be cleaned before they reach the air. A wet scrubber does it by bringing the gas into close contact with a liquid that absorbs or neutralises it.

The journey of the gas

  1. A centrifugal blower moves the fumes from the plant, through ducting, into the scrubber.
  2. The gas enters low on the column and rises through a packed bed: a deep layer of plastic packing with a very large surface.
  3. Spray nozzles at the top wet the packing with scrubbing liquid, which runs down as the gas rises, so the two meet across the whole bed.
  4. Above the sprays, a mist eliminator catches the fine droplets the gas would otherwise carry out.
  5. The cleaned gas leaves through the outlet at the top.

The liquid side

The scrubbing liquid collects in the sump at the bottom of the column, and a recirculation pump sends it back up to the spray header. Part of it is bled off and replaced as it becomes loaded with what it has absorbed.

In shortGas up, liquid down, across a large wet surface: the fumes are absorbed or neutralised on the way through.

Why plastic and FRP

Every part of a scrubber is in contact with corrosive gas or liquid. That is why we build them in PP, PVC and FRP rather than metal, with the blower, ducting and pumps in matching materials.
