PP, HDPE, PVC or PVDF? Choosing a thermoplastic for your chemical

The material decides how long equipment survives the chemical inside it. Here is how we think about the choice.

Plasto-Fab team · 2 min read

No single plastic is right for every duty. The choice depends on what the equipment will hold, how hot it gets, and the loads it has to carry. These are the materials we weld most often, and where each one fits.

PP-H: polypropylene

Resistant to a wide range of acids, alkalis and salt solutions, and rigid enough for tanks and vessels. It is our most common choice for storage and process tanks, scrubbers and ducting.

HDPE: high-density polyethylene

Tough, impact-resistant and chemically inert. A natural choice for effluent treatment and large storage tanks.

PVC-U and CPVC

PVC-U is rigid, with high mechanical strength, for structural tanks and pipes. CPVC keeps its strength at higher temperatures than PVC-U, for hot process liquids.

PVDF and ECTFE

Fluoropolymers for the hardest duties: strong acids, halogens, chlorine and higher temperatures, where the standard plastics are not enough.

When one material is not enough

When the chemical calls for a thermoplastic but the equipment is large, or carries high loads, we put a thermoplastic liner inside a glass-fibre reinforced shell. That is a dual laminate: how it works.

What we need to knowThe chemical and its concentration, and anything else in the stream; the operating temperature and its peaks; any pressure or vacuum; the size, and whether it stands indoors or outdoors.

Send us those, and we will recommend the material, and the construction, that will last. You can also compare the materials we build with.
