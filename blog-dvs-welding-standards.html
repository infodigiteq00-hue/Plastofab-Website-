DVS 2207 and 2208: what the welding guidelines mean for your equipment

The German standards behind every thermoplastic weld we make, and why they matter to the people who buy the equipment.

Plasto-Fab team · 2 min read

DVS, the German Welding Society, publishes the guidelines that thermoplastic fabricators around the world work to. Two series matter most for tanks, scrubbers and ducting.

DVS 2207: how the weld is made

DVS 2207 covers the welding of thermoplastics: processes such as heated-tool butt welding and extrusion welding, for materials including PE, PP and PVDF. It sets out how the joint is prepared, and the temperatures, pressures and times each material needs.

DVS 2208: the machines that make it

DVS 2208 covers the machines and devices used to weld thermoplastics, so the equipment itself delivers the conditions the welding guidelines call for. Ours is German-made Wegener & Widos equipment.

In short2207 is how to weld; 2208 is what to weld with. Together they make a good weld repeatable.

Why it matters to you

  • Repeatable welds: the same parameters, joint after joint.
  • Joints made for a long life, which is what the guidelines exist for.
  • A common language: you can name DVS on your enquiry and know what you are getting.

Where it started

Plasto-Fab was founded in 1992 by Mr. Mehul Pandya, a DVS-certified plastic welder. Welding to the standard has been part of how we work from the first day. See how we build on our fabrication page.
